# 01 — Principles and Decision Records

**Read with:** `00_INDEX.md`. Evidence for the decisions is in `05_RESEARCH_NOTES.md`.

---

## 1. Principles

Every design choice in this folder follows from these five. When a spec is silent, decide by them.

1. **The ledger is the source of truth; weights are a cache of it.** Every real input is stored.
   Every slab records which entries trained it and can be rebuilt from them.
2. **One substrate, several clocks.** Fast memory and long-term memory are the same object (a
   low-rank slab) updated at different frequencies.
3. **The frozen base is the only teacher.** Synthetic training data is written by the frozen base
   while it reads real entries. The adapted model never trains on its own output.
4. **A slab's behaviour must not depend on which other slabs are loaded.** The set of loaded slabs
   changes every turn. This holds exactly for a slab's gate weight. It holds for its applied
   contribution only while the top-k cap does not bind; the cap is a bounded, measured exception
   (D-28, D-34).
5. **Nothing is committed without a functional test.** Every write, merge, prune and rebuild is
   checked against held-out probes and rolled back on failure.

---

## 2. Decision records

Status values: `decided` (follow it), `needs-test` (follow it until the named experiment reports,
then confirm or replace), `superseded`.

### D-01 Ledger as source of truth
- **Decision:** All real inputs go to an append-only local store. Slabs are derived artefacts.
- **Why:** Gives forgetting by rebuild with known provenance (the guarantees and their limits are
  in D-35 and `P08` §3), rollback, merge verification and base-model replacement. The PRD has no
  mechanism for any of these.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-02 Fast slab replaces a Titans-style associative memory (PRD Tier 1)
- **Decision:** Tier 1 is a rank-16 slab updated by a few gradient steps per turn.
- **Why:** Titans and Hope train their memory projections together with the backbone. Nested
  Learning's LLM experiment continues pre-training for 15B tokens; no frozen-base variant exists.
  Untrained projections attached to a frozen model inject noise. The PRD's own deprecation log
  also drops token-level fast-weight updates.
- **Status:** decided. The fast slab itself is `needs-test` (experiment E-L1, P2): if it adds
  nothing over recall, the per-turn clock is removed.

### D-03 Frozen base is the only teacher
- **Decision:** Derived samples are generated by the base with slabs disabled, reading cited
  ledger entries in context.
- **Why:** Removes the recursive self-training loop that causes model collapse. Makes a derived
  sample a function of its cited entries only, so a slab rebuilt without an entry has no
  first-order dependence on it (the limits are stated in `P08` §3).
- **Status:** decided.

### D-04 Independent per-slab gates
- **Decision:** Each slab's gate is a sigmoid of its own row-space energy. No normalisation across
  slabs. A top-k cap bounds how many contribute.
- **Why:** MoRA normalises scores across all ranks. With a hot set that changes per turn, that
  makes a slab's output depend on unrelated slabs (violates principle 4).
- **Evidence gap:** MoRA's results are for competitive gating. Independent gating is untested.
- **Status:** needs-test (experiment E-GATE, P1). Fallbacks, in order: keyed gate; competitive gate
  within a fixed training-time hot set.

### D-05 Functional tests gate every commit
- **Decision:** New-probe recall, neighbour retention, anchor KL, gate leak and the safety
  measures (D-39) are checked before any slab version goes live.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-06 Commits only at safe points
- **Decision:** A trained shadow slab goes live only at session start, at context eviction, or
  after an idle re-prefill.
- **Why:** Changing weights invalidates the KV cache computed under the old weights. The PRD's
  pointer swap does not avoid this.
- **Status:** decided. Stale commits are `needs-test` (experiment E-STALE, P2) and are off by default.

### D-07 Retrieval is a first-class path
- **Decision:** Ledger chunks are recalled into context every turn.
- **Why:** SEAL's best no-context recall after training on a passage is 47.0%. A systematic study
  of parametric RAG finds it loses fine-grained detail and works best combined with retrieval.
  Exact strings should come from recall; slabs carry vocabulary, style, skills and frequent knowledge.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-08 Time as text; supersession instead of decay
- **Decision:** Dates are plain text in samples, recall output and the system prompt. A newer
  contradicting entry supersedes an older one. Only the fast slab decays.
- **Why:** Age does not make a fact false. Blanket decay deletes facts that are still true.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-09 Forget by rebuild
- **Decision:** Forgetting a personal fact rebuilds the affected slabs without it. A trained
  `refusal` slab is used only to suppress knowledge the base itself holds.
- **Why:** TOFU finds gradient-based unlearning weak and damaging. For personal facts the frozen
  base is already the calibrated "does not know" state.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-10 Real-data rule
- **Decision:** Every training batch is at least 25% real tokens. Derived samples have generation
  depth 1. All real data is kept permanently.
- **Why:** The PRD's 2% figure has no support. Accumulating real data bounds collapse error
  (Gerstgrasser et al.); D-03 removes the loop itself.
- **Status:** decided. 25% is a starting value.

### D-11 Merge by co-activation and effective rank
- **Decision:** Merge candidates are slabs that fire together or have near centroids. They are
  packed without loss when their effective ranks fit in one slab; otherwise a merged slab is
  retrained from the ledger and accepted only if probes hold.
- **Why:** The PRD's weight-cosine > 0.92 rule would practically never fire: independent rank-8
  updates measured max |cos| 0.0011. Truncating two unrelated rank-8 slabs to rank 8 keeps 55% of
  the energy. Entry-wise merging methods (DARE, TIES) operate on dense matrices and destroy
  low-rank structure.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-12 Context-distillation training objective
- **Decision:** The slab is trained so the model without the source matches the base with the
  source in context (KL on answer tokens), plus raw-text CE, replay and anchor terms.
- **Why:** Reported to beat next-token training for knowledge adapters (Caccia et al.).
- **Status:** decided. Hidden-state matching is experiment E-HID (P3).

### D-13 Attachment points
- **Decision:** `q_proj` and `v_proj` in every layer.
- **Why:** All-layer attachment avoids late-layer-only plasticity. Attention projections are small.
- **Status:** decided. MLP attachment is experiment E-MLP (P3).

### D-14 Separate sentence encoder for recall and routing
- **Decision:** A small frozen sentence-embedding model produces chunk embeddings and slab centroids.
- **Why:** Embeddings must not change when slabs change. Mean-pooled causal-LM states retrieve poorly.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-15 Single process, two threads, plain PyTorch
- **Decision:** One serving thread and one worker thread share the base weights. No vLLM, no
  shared-memory ring buffer, no RPC.
- **Why:** One to seven users. vLLM does not support per-token gated adapters. The ledger replaces
  the ring buffer.
- **Status:** decided. Revisit if a second GPU is dedicated to training.

### D-16 Safety as a behavioural commit gate
- **Decision:** Refusal behaviour is protected by a KL-to-base term on a refusal probe set and by
  a behavioural commit test (three measures since D-39).
- **Why:** The alternative, projecting gradients orthogonal to a refusal direction, does not
  guarantee unchanged behaviour in a non-linear network: other layers can change the activations
  feeding that direction. Behaviour has to be measured.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-17 Tool syntax by constrained decoding
- **Decision:** JSON validity comes from grammar-constrained decoding. `exec` slabs improve tool
  and argument choice.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-18 Development on a 0.5B base on CPU
- **Decision:** All mechanisms are built and tested on Qwen2.5-0.5B before any GPU work.
- **Why:** The available machine has no discrete GPU.
- **Consequence:** A 0.5B teacher writes poor derived samples. The evaluation harness has an
  `oracle` teacher mode that isolates this. See D-42 for what `dev` results do and do not show.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-19 OOV handling is optional and last
- **Decision:** Input-side embedding table, built in P5, shipped only if it beats its ablation.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-20 No closed-form edits to base weights
- **Decision:** Closed-form rank-1 edits of a base projection matrix (ROME-style) are not used.
- **Why:** They modify the frozen base. Their locality relies on keys of unrelated facts being
  near-orthogonal, which real hidden states are not. Fact updates are served immediately by
  supersession plus recall.
- **Status:** decided. A gated rank-1 *edit slab* is listed as extension X-3 in `P12`.

### D-21 Sealing as the stability mechanism
- **Decision:** A slab stops training when it reaches its token capacity. Sealed slabs receive no
  gradients.
- **Why:** Knowledge that no gradient touches cannot be overwritten. Interference can then only
  come through gating, which replay and retention tests cover.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-22 Slabs operate on standardised inputs
- **Decision:** Each adapted module's input is standardised with fixed per-dimension mean and
  standard deviation measured once on the frozen base (`P02` §2.1). Gate and update both use it.
- **Why:** Raised in external review (2026-11-11). Hidden states are anisotropic; a few
  high-variance dimensions and a large mean would open raw-energy gates on generic text. The
  statistics are frozen, not running, so committed slabs do not drift.
- **Evidence gap:** anisotropy of Qwen 2.5 projection inputs has not been measured here
  (I-P02-12 does it). Standardisation does not remove correlations; an optional deflation of
  the top principal directions is an E-GATE arm (`slabs.gate_deflate_rank`, `P02` §2.1).
- **Status:** needs-test (E-GATE compares `standardize` with `none`).

### D-23 CPU contention: time-slicing or a worker process
- **Decision:** `runtime.worker_mode` is `thread` with strict time-slicing and capped step time,
  or `process` with its own thread count and affinity (`P04` §7.1).
- **Why:** Raised in external review. PyTorch's intra-op thread count is process-wide, so two
  threads in one process cannot be given separate pools; the choice is not to overlap, or to
  separate processes.
- **Status:** decided; measured by G-5.

### D-24 No cross-slab replay by default
- **Decision:** `L_replay` uses only the training slab's own earlier samples. Neighbour replay is
  a recorded fallback (`role='replay'` in `slab_sources`).
- **Why:** Raised in external review. Replayed neighbour samples put one slab's entries into
  another slab's training batches without recording it, which made the forget guarantee false
  as written and would force multi-slab rebuilds.
- **Status:** needs-test (E-REPLAY).

### D-25 Rule-based cloze and starvation fallback for weak teachers
- **Decision:** Code-generated cloze samples are a first-class sample source; when the teacher's
  acceptance rate is below `teacher.min_accept_rate`, entries are trained from them alone. The
  consistency filter is off on `dev`.
- **Why:** Raised in external review. A 0.5B teacher will fail the filters most of the time.
- **Limit:** rule-based cloze teaches spans that are literally in the text. It cannot teach
  implications, paraphrase or multi-entry knowledge; those need a capable teacher (D-42).
- **Status:** decided. Filter settings and acceptance rate are stated in every report.

### D-26 Lineage and concatenation in compaction
- **Decision:** Slabs record a `cluster_id`; compaction prefers same-cluster pairs; pairs that do
  not fit one slab are concatenated into a multi-block slab under one gate before any lossy merge.
  `compact.pack_cos` is renamed `compact.pack_centroid_cos`.
- **Why:** External review read `pack_cos` as weight cosine (it is source-text similarity) and
  argued packing would never fire. The misreading showed the name was unclear; the worry that
  lossless packing is rare is fair. Concatenation is weight-exact and always available
  (gating is not preserved; see D-45).
- **Consequence:** total parameters on disk are not bounded; memory in use is (`P07` §9).
- **Status:** decided.

### D-27 Session pinning
- **Decision:** No slab is unloaded while a session holds a KV cache; overflow triggers a
  re-prefill (`P04` §5).
- **Why:** Raised in external review. Swapping a slab out leaves cached keys and values that
  were computed with it.
- **Status:** decided. Adding a slab mid-session remains an approximation (E-STALE).

### Review items not adopted
- **Soft-prefix or key-value token cache in place of the fast slab.** A learned prefix needs its
  own training and also changes the KV cache; plain retrieval into context already provides
  within-session recall. The observation behind it (L1 cannot help the triggering session) is
  correct and now stated in `P05` §1.
- **Per-thread `torch.set_num_threads` partitioning inside one process.** Not possible as
  described; see D-23.
- **Full covariance whitening `Σ^{-1/2}`.** A `d×d` matrix per module is too large; diagonal
  standardisation is used (D-22).

### D-28 Calibrated cap ranking; the cap is a safety bound
- **Decision:** The top-k cap ranks slabs by margin standardised with each slab's own frozen
  reference statistics (`m_ref`). The cap-binding rate is measured and reported.
- **Why:** Second external review (2026-11-11). Raw margins of separately trained slabs are not
  on a common scale, so a raw top-k is biased toward whichever slab has the larger scale. The
  review's "permanent starvation" is overstated (the cap only acts when more than `k` gates are
  open on one token), but the bias is real and the cap is where principle 4 can break.
- **Not adopted:** running z-scores (they would drift committed behaviour); removing the cap
  (unbounded sums of updates).
- **Status:** needs-test (E-GATE reports margin spread and cap-binding rate).

### D-29 Retraction instead of differentiating through QR
- **Decision:** `A` is re-orthonormalised after every optimiser step, with `R` folded into `B`
  and first-moment buffers moved into the new basis. No gradient passes through QR or a
  triangular solve.
- **Why:** Second review: differentiating through `R⁻ᵀ` is fragile if `A` loses rank. Also
  resolves the momentum-basis mismatch at canonicalisation that the review raised for the fast slab.
- **Correction to the review:** its claim that `d(log e)/de = 1/e` explodes at the clamp floor
  ignores the sigmoid; the product is bounded when `τ ≤ 1`. That constraint is now enforced.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-30 One gate function for training and inference
- **Decision:** `w = max(0, (sigmoid(m) − δ)/(1 − δ))` everywhere; no inference-only cut-off.
  New slabs start with the gate open (`phi_init = −1.1`).
- **Why:** Second review: an inference-only cut creates a mismatch and a jump at the boundary.
  Working through the fix showed a further problem the review did not raise: with the old
  closed-start initialisation a new slab (`B = 0`) would sit in the zero region and get no gradient.
- **Status:** needs-test (E-GATE compares open-start and closed-start).

### D-31 Greedy rollouts gate commits
- **Decision:** Commit test 6 decodes probes greedily and fails on low match or any degenerate output.
- **Why:** Second review: teacher-forced NLL can pass while free generation loops or stops early.
  This reverses the earlier rule that generation metrics never gate a commit.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-32 Routing by recall hits and multiple centroids
- **Decision:** A turn loads first the slabs whose source entries were recalled, then slabs
  ranked by their best of up to four k-means centroids.
- **Why:** Second review: one mean centroid over 100–200 entries is too generic for niche
  queries. (Its "norm decay" point does not apply; centroids were already normalised.) Recall
  hits go further than the review's fix: retrieval has already located the exact entries.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-33 Ledger write discipline and block embedding store
- **Decision:** Busy timeout on every connection; no model work inside a transaction; bulk
  writes in small batches; embeddings in fixed blocks with a validity mask and zero-on-remove.
- **Why:** Second review. Its stated mechanism (FTS5 blocking readers in WAL mode) did not
  reproduce: 1,438 reads, no errors, 14 ms maximum during a 20,000-row FTS transaction. The same
  test showed the real problem: a second writer fails immediately without a busy timeout and
  waited 1.7 s with one, and the serving thread writes every turn.
- **Status:** decided.

### Second review: items not adopted
- **Position padding or position-id override for distillation.** The premise (hidden states
  carry rotary phases that the slab must model) does not hold for rotary encoding, which acts on
  relative offsets inside attention, and the loss compares output distributions. Padding the
  student with dummy tokens would change its context. A check is added instead (I-P03-10), with
  the override as the remedy if a base model fails it.
- **Changing the default attachment away from `q_proj`, `v_proj`.** The grouped-attention
  observation is correct but its effect is an empirical question; E-MLP now compares five
  attachment sets.

### D-34 Gate weight and applied weight are separate quantities; the cap is tested under overload
- **Decision:** Every spec distinguishes the **gate weight** `w` (a function of the token and the
  slab alone) from the **applied weight** `ŵ` (after the top-k cap, which depends on the other
  open slabs). Both are collected. E-GATE gains an overload condition in which more than `k`
  slabs are open on the same tokens, and a pass condition on the cap-binding rate
  (`slabs.max_cap_binding`). If the condition fails, the selection strategy is chosen from the
  alternatives listed in `P12` E-GATE and recorded here.
- **Why:** Third external review (2026-11-12), issue 1. The claim checked out in part: `P02` §4.2
  and D-28 already said the cap is where principle 4 can break, and I-P02-5 and I-P02-16 already
  test both sides of it. What was missing: the glossary and principle 4 used one word for both
  quantities, E-GATE did not force the cap to bind, and "a few percent" was not a threshold.
- **Status:** needs-test (E-GATE).

### D-35 Three forget guarantees, stated separately
- **Decision:** "Forgotten" means three things with different strength (`P08` §3): **source
  removal** and **provenance removal** are guaranteed by construction and checked by tests;
  **behavioural removal** is measured against the bare base on every forget and reported, never
  inferred from a successful rebuild. The term "exact unlearning" is no longer used for this
  system. Forget also searches for other entries that restate the fact and offers them.
- **Why:** Third review, issue 2. Checked and true: `00` §4, D-01 and `06` C-4 said "exact
  unlearning", which holds only with respect to the listed sources. A second entry that repeats
  the fact survives, and so does any sample derived from it. Of the review's five reasons, two
  were already handled (multi-entry samples are deleted with any cited entry; replayed entries
  are in `slab_sources`), two are real (repeated information, correlated learning in other
  slabs), one is the already-stated second-order limit.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-36 One primary owner per entry; replay dependencies are separate
- **Decision:** Every consolidated entry has exactly one `primary` row among non-retired level ≥ 2
  slabs and any number of `replay` rows. `unconsolidated` and I-P06-4 are defined on primary rows
  of non-retired slabs.
- **Why:** Third review, issue 3. Checked and true: I-P06-4 counted all `slab_sources` rows, which
  contradicts the replay retry (`P06` §4) and I-P08-8. Checking it exposed two further faults the
  review did not raise: a packed slab and its retired parents both list the same entries, and
  entries of a retired slab were never returned by `unconsolidated`, so the "next consolidation
  re-assigns them" step of `P08` §5 could not happen.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-37 Feasibility is measured before the training pipeline is built
- **Decision:** Task T0.8 measures generation and prefill throughput, forward-plus-backward step
  time, peak memory and disk use on the `dev` machine, and projects the wall time of one L1
  write, one consolidation cycle and each required run. Gate G-0 records the projection. A
  required run projected over `eval.run_budget_hours` needs a decision before P1 starts.
- **Why:** Third review, issue 4. Checked and true: the specs name the hardware limit but contain
  no measurement of it, and `P11` §8 fixes run sizes of 50 and 200 cycles.
- **Status:** decided. The budget is a planning value the owner may change.

### D-38 Erasure policy: logical, database-level, media-level
- **Decision:** The ledger runs with `secure_delete` on and FTS5 `secure-delete` on. A forget is
  not reported done until a truncating WAL checkpoint has succeeded and a byte search of the
  data directory's files for the entry's canary strings finds nothing. Erasure from the storage
  medium (file-system slack, SSD remapping, snapshots, backups, swap) is **not** guaranteed and
  is said so to the user; an encrypted volume is the recommended setting.
- **Why:** Third review, issue 5. Checked and true, by test
  (`reference/sqlite_forget_residue.py`, `05` §3.5): after the logical wipe the text stayed in
  the WAL and the database file; with `secure_delete` alone it stayed in the FTS5 index; a reader
  holding a snapshot blocked the truncating checkpoint. With both settings and a completed
  checkpoint no canary remained. I-P08-1 as written was not met by the specified operations.
- **Not adopted:** per-entry encryption keys (cryptographic erasure). Standard `sqlite3` has no
  encryption, and recall needs plaintext indexes; listed as X-7 in `P12`.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-39 Safety gate measures three things and does not rest on the phrase classifier alone
- **Decision:** The commit test reports and gates **refusal preservation**, **benign
  preservation** and **harmful-content leakage** separately. Leakage is scored on the full reply
  by the frozen base's own likelihood of it, not by phrase patterns. `base_refuses` is
  hand-labelled for the `eval` split. Paraphrase and adversarial variants are part of the probe
  file. A human audit of full replies is required at each phase exit that claims G-8.
- **Why:** Third review, issue 6. Checked and true: `is_refusal` read the first sentence of a
  24-token reply, so a reply that refuses and then complies passed, and a benign reply with a
  refusal phrase failed. The agreement figure is agreement with the base under a classifier; it
  is not a safety rate and is no longer described as one.
- **Status:** decided. Thresholds are starting values.

### D-40 Clustered bootstrap; baselines reproduced before any public claim
- **Decision:** Intervals are computed by a paired bootstrap that resamples **clusters of
  questions sharing an underlying fact**, per seed, with seeds reported separately. A claim
  reaches `demonstrated` only against baselines this project reproduced under a protocol recorded
  in `P13` §6; published numbers are context, never the comparison.
- **Why:** Third review, issue 7. Part A checked and true: the same fact is asked at several
  lags, with recall on and off, and inside two-hop questions, so questions are not independent.
  Part B: `P13` §3 already required same-model baselines, but `06` §3 item 2 still allowed
  published numbers to stand in; that alternative is removed.
- **Status:** decided. The cluster definition is refined by D-49.

### D-41 Kill criterion G-K: one rule, one predeclared primary metric
- **Decision:** The primary metric is the **unweighted mean of recall-on accuracy on `jargon`,
  `style` and `multi`**, the categories that justify parametric memory (D-07). G-K passes when
  `full` is above `a` on that metric (clustered-bootstrap 95% interval excluding zero on
  every seed, `P11` §5.1) and is not worse than `a` on any single category. Per-category
  differences are secondary: they are reported and set the per-category status of C-8 in `06`,
  but they do not decide G-K.
- **Why:** Third review, issue 8. Checked and true: the gate table read as "all three" and the
  explanatory text as "any". The review recommends a predeclared primary metric and threshold
  on the categories that justify parametric memory. One test on their mean does that and avoids both
  faults of the alternatives: "any one of three" gives three chances to pass by noise, and "all
  three" kills the work when one small category is inconclusive.
- **Status:** decided by the spec author on 2026-11-12; **the owner should confirm** before T3.6.

### D-42 Deployment target and the role of the `dev` machine
- **Decision:** The system is meant to run on a GPU with enough memory, on Qwen, DeepSeek or
  another supported family at roughly 8B to 32B parameters or more (owner, 2026-11-12). The
  `dev` laptop with the 0.5B base is for building and checking mechanics. Results on `dev` in
  `teacher.mode: model` are not evidence about learning quality at deployment size, in either
  direction. Runs that close a gate at the listed size may be run on the GPU profile; `dev`
  runs of reduced size are labelled as mechanics checks.
- **Why:** Third review, issues 4 and the teacher concern. A 0.5B teacher cannot write useful
  semantic samples and a CPU cannot time-box 50- or 200-cycle runs; neither says anything about
  the design at its intended size. T0.8 still measures `dev`, so that the split between what is
  run where is decided from numbers.
- **Status:** decided. Extends D-18.

### Third review: engineering concerns (section 3 of the review)
- **P07, concatenation does not bound parameters or disk.** True and already stated (D-26,
  `P07` §9). Adopted the review's framing: two separate resource guarantees, bounded inference
  residency and unbounded archival storage with a measured growth rate (`P07` §9, G-9).
- **P09, unsupported JSON Schema weakens the guarantee.** True. Each tool now has a recorded
  constraint level; a call for a tool outside the subset is validated after decoding and is
  never executed if it fails (`P09` §6).
- **P04, mid-session slab loads need stronger evaluation.** True: E-STALE measured one reply at
  100 points. It now varies depth, number of slabs and turns after the load, checks rollouts,
  and has a suffix re-prefill arm (`P12`).
- **P03, a 0.5B teacher and rule-based cloze cannot give semantic learning.** True. Stated as a
  limit in `P03` §4; `dev` evaluates `multi` and `implication` learning in oracle mode only (D-42).
- **P02, standardisation does not whiten.** True and already stated (D-22). Added an
  experimental arm that removes the top principal directions from the gate (`P02` §2.1,
  E-GATE); full whitening stays not adopted.
- **P05, L1 may add little over retrieval.** True and already stated (`P05` §1); E-L1 decides.
  No change.

### D-43 The gate ramp is kept; dead gates are detected and reset
- **Decision:** `w = max(0, (s − δ)/(1 − δ))` stays. `train_slab` resets `φ` when a slab's gate is
  0 on every own-sample token of a batch. The text on gradient bounds in `P02` §4.1 is corrected.
- **Why:** Fourth external review (2026-11-12), items 1A and 1B, each checked.
  *1A:* the review's formula is right (`dw/de ≈ 1,154` at the cut) and its conclusion is not. The
  number is large because `e` is about `1.7e-4` there; in log-lift units the slope is `δ/τ = 0.2`,
  and the gradient reaching `A` is at most 52 per token, with no overflow in any dtype. The cut
  is a kink like a ReLU's. The review is right that the old text argued about `e → 0`, which the
  cut makes irrelevant; that text is fixed.
  *1B:* the stated mechanism does not occur. With `B = 0` the anchor and safety losses are at
  their minimum and give `φ` no gradient, and at `lr_slab = 1e-4` `φ` cannot move the 2.2 units
  needed in a few steps. The end state is real, though: a fully closed gate has no gradient and
  nothing detected it before the commit test failed.
- **Not adopted:** a smooth (`C¹`) ramp; freezing `φ` during warm-up by default. A slower `φ`
  schedule is an E-GATE arm.
- **Status:** decided; E-GATE reports dead-gate resets.

### D-44 Retraction details: positive `R`, no row-space step, decay on `B` only
- **Decision:** QR results are sign-normalised so `R` has a positive diagonal. The part of each
  `A` step that lies in the old row space is dropped before retraction. Weight decay and the
  fast slab's decay apply to `B` only.
- **Why:** Fourth review, items 1C and 1E, and runtime item 2B. Checked numerically (`05` §3.6):
  a canonical `A` that came from an SVD gave a negative diagonal in every trial, so `R ≈ I` was
  false after prune or pack; dropping the row-space part cuts `|R − I|` by a factor of ten;
  decaying both factors decays the update by `(1 − γ)²` and leaves `A` unchanged.
  Corrections to the review: `B A` was preserved even with the wrong sign, and a row-space step
  does not "pump" `‖B‖` artificially, it is simply a direction `B` already owns. The claim that
  weight decay erodes old facts "over months" does not apply to sealed slabs, which are never
  stepped; weight decay was unspecified and is now 0 by default.
- **Status:** decided. Adam's second moments remain untransformed (D-29).

### D-45 Packing is weight-exact, not behaviour-exact
- **Decision:** No merge is called lossless. Pack, concatenate and prune are accepted only if a
  cross-talk check passes: KL between the model with the parents and with the merged slab, on
  each parent's probes and on anchor text, at most `compact.max_crosstalk_kl`.
- **Why:** Fourth review, item 1D. True: one gate replaces two, so the merged slab applies one
  parent's update on tokens where only the other used to fire. The retention test already ran,
  but the specs said "no loss of content" and I-P07-8 claimed exactness without qualification.
- **Status:** decided. The threshold is a starting value.

### D-46 One slot pool, masked per session; sessions are parked, never overwritten
- **Decision:** A forward pass applies only the current session's slabs and shared namespaces.
  When the pool is full, another session is parked (cache dropped, slabs released).
- **Why:** Fourth review, item 2A. The review's scenario (one user's slots overwritten under a
  live cache) was already forbidden by D-27, but what should happen instead was not specified.
  Checking it showed something worse that the review did not raise: with several sessions
  live, one user's private slabs stayed loaded and their gates could open on another user's
  tokens.
- **Also:** context eviction now removes the dropped turns' chunks from the session's recalled
  set (review item 2E, true).
- **Status:** decided.

### D-47 Write transactions are immediate and contain no file I/O; slab files are versioned
- **Decision:** Every ledger write uses `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`. Files are placed before the
  transaction and cleaned up after it. Slab files carry their version in the name.
- **Why:** Fourth review, items 2B and 2D. 2D checked by test: a transaction that reads and then
  writes failed at once, with a 5 s timeout set, after another connection committed
  (`05` §3.6); `append` and `bump_access` have that shape. 2B is true and was introduced by the
  previous revision of `P07` §5 (a file copy inside the commit transaction). Versioned names
  make the crash cases recoverable: the ledger never points at a missing or half-replaced file.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-48 A superseding entry takes over its predecessor's slab
- **Decision:** The rebuild triggered by a supersession includes the new entry, and links it to
  the old entry's primary owner when the rebuild commits.
- **Why:** Fourth review, item 2C. True in substance: the rebuilt slab held neither value until
  a later consolidation assigned the new entry somewhere. The window was one consolidation
  cycle, not "days", and recall served the new value throughout. The review's fix (link inside
  `mark_superseded`) is not used: linking before training would record knowledge the weights do
  not have (see `P04` §6.1).
- **Status:** decided.

### D-49 Clusters by answer object; one corrected guard
- **Decision:** A question's bootstrap cluster is the object its answer belongs to, with no
  chaining. The "not worse on any category" guard is one family of tests on seed-averaged
  differences with Holm correction at 5%.
- **Why:** Fourth review, items 3A and 3B. 3A: with the two-hop template in the spec, components
  were one project each, not the 60–80% component the review predicts; but nothing bounded the
  templates, and a person owning several projects would have linked them. 3B checked: 21
  uncorrected checks give a 41% false-failure rate. Holm is chosen over the review's
  Benjamini-Hochberg because one rejection fails the guard, so the family-wise rate is the one
  that matters.
- **Status:** decided. Refines D-40 and D-41.

### D-50 Gate arithmetic in float32; floor on calibration spread; GPU hazards registered
- **Decision:** Standardisation, norms, energies and margins are computed in `float32` on every
  profile. The cap ranking uses `max(std, slabs.m_ref_std_floor)`. `gpu24` starts at a 16,384
  token context. The remaining GPU hazards are listed in `P04` §11 with what T5.1 must measure.
- **Why:** Fourth review, part 2. The precision point follows from `bfloat16` having 8
  significant bits and needs no GPU to see. The calibration example in the review is wrong (a
  token far below a narrow slab's mean ranks very low, not high), but a tiny spread does turn
  small differences into decisive ones, so the floor is adopted. The KV-cache arithmetic was
  recomputed (6.0 GiB at 32k). Fragmentation and launch overhead are plausible and unmeasured;
  the specific remedies and figures are recorded as unverified.
- **Status:** decided for precision and the floor; the rest needs-test (T5.1).

### D-51 Ledger: user-scoped candidate lists, a FIFO writer lock, and rules enforced in code (T0.3)
- **Decision:** (1) Recall restricts both candidate lists to the user's `active`/`pending` entries
  and drops excluded chunk ids *before* taking the top 50, not after (`P01` §4.4). The in-memory
  index carries an owner code per row for this. "Or is shared" is removed from §4.4: no spec
  defines a shared entry, so recall is per user. (2) In-process writers queue on a FIFO lock
  before `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` (`P01` §4.6). (3) The rules of `P01` §3.1 and §4.6 that were tests
  only are enforced in code: a write outside `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, an encoder or tokenizer call
  inside a write transaction, a derived row citing an untrainable or non-live entry, and linking
  such an entry to a slab all raise. (4) `Ledger` takes the config as a fourth argument.
- **Why:** (1) With several users the global top 50 can hold none of one user's chunks, so the
  dense list would be empty for them although the data exists. (2) SQLite's busy handler polls
  with growing sleeps and is not fair. Measured: with the lock removed, a chat append made one
  attempt during a 2,000-chunk file ingest and starved (I-P01-8 failed, 1 turn instead of 250);
  with it the slowest chat append was 16 ms. Cross-process writers still rely on the busy
  timeout and `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`; two `Ledger` objects on one database did 2 x 400 read-then-write
  appends with no lock error. (3) Hard rules 4 and 5 and D-47 are cheaper to keep when the
  ledger refuses the call than when a test notices later. (4) `ledger.*` and `recall.*` keys
  must reach the ledger (`02` §4: no literals).
- **Evidence:** `tests/unit/test_ledger*.py`, `test_embedding_store.py`; mutation checks run by
  hand: removing the user filter, the status filter, the FIFO lock, the zeroing of removed rows,
  or the checkpoint each fails a named test.
- **Also measured:** with FTS5 `secure-delete` on, FTS5's own `integrity-check` command reports
  `database disk image is malformed` after any delete, although every query is correct (0 of 40
  terms disagreed with the content table after 120 deletes); `optimize` clears it. Do not use
  `integrity-check` as a health check between optimises (`05` §3.5).
- **Status:** decided.

### D-52 Engine details fixed at T0.5
- **Decision:** (1) The recall for a turn excludes chunks of entries that are already in the
  session's context verbatim (its own user messages and replies), as well as chunks already
  recalled into it. (2) The user's words are always wrapped in `<message>`; `<memory>` appears
  only when there are chunks. (3) The KV cache covers `token_ids[:kv_len]`; the closing tokens of
  an assistant turn are appended without a forward pass and enter the cache with the next
  prefill. (4) A turn that fails or whose reply is abandoned is rolled back: tokens removed, cache
  discarded (re-prefill next turn); the user's message stays in the ledger. (5) `Engine` takes the
  model, tokenizer, ledger, embedder and clock as optional arguments, so tests can inject the
  `tiny` model; with none given it loads from the config. (6) `session_id=None` means the user's
  current session; a session that crosses midnight is restarted under the same id.
- **Why:** (1) Without it a turn's memory block repeats the conversation it sits in. Measured on
  the real base: the first run recalled the assistant's own earlier reply into the next turn and
  the 0.5B model copied it. Replies remain recallable across sessions (L-2). (3) Saves a forward
  over two tokens per turn and keeps `kv_len` the single truth about the cache. (4) The ledger
  must keep every real input (hard rule 3), but the context must match its cache.
- **Evidence:** `tests/unit/test_engine.py` (tiny): I-P04-5, 6, 8, 13 and I-P11-3; eight
  deliberate breakages each fail a test. `tests/slow/test_engine_real.py` (Qwen2.5-0.5B on CPU):
  incremental vs full-forward logits differ by at most 5.9e-5 with the same argmax; about 15
  tokens/s decode including recall.
- **Status:** decided.

### D-53 Synthetic-user details fixed at T0.6
- **Decision:** (1) **I-P11-8 (10% cap on the largest cluster)** is enforced for categories with at
  least 20 clusters in the stream. For a category with fewer clusters the cap cannot be met: with
  `n` clusters the largest holds at least `1/n` of the questions (style: 10 rules, so at least
  10%, and exactly 10% only when every rule is asked equally often; tool: 5 tools, at least
  20%). Their figure is still computed and written to the report, and §5.1's rule stands that a
  category with fewer than 10 clusters is reported without pass/fail and left out of the guard.
  (2) **Stream shape lives in `StreamParams`** (turn-type weights, session and turn counts, restate
  share, the forget-twice share, how early jargon and (owner, port) pairs arrive), not in the
  config: they describe the benchmark, are fixed with the seed, and are written into `truth.json`.
  The weights give about 1.3 new facts per cycle, so the world's ~270 stated facts last 200
  cycles. (3) **Jargon facts and each project's (owner, port) pair arrive earlier on average**
  (a priority of 0.35 on a random sort key), so that `jargon` and `multi` reach 10 clusters in a
  50-cycle run. (4) **A forget request names its entry by date, object and attribute, never the
  value**, so the request entry holds nothing the byte search would flag. Only facts stated in
  chat turns and never updated can be forgotten (a file entry holds ~10 other facts). The share
  of forgets that target a twice-stated fact is exact in sequence: request `k` is of that kind
  when `floor((k+1)·0.4) > floor(k·0.4)`; when no such fact exists yet the turn restates a fact
  instead and the same `k` is tried again. Facts stated twice are not updated. (5) **The probe
  paraphrase set** that §2.3 calls "a further held-out set" is two paraphrases per attribute,
  separate from the three question paraphrases. (6) The anchor questions asked in evaluation are
  a fixed list of 40, none of which is in `anchor_seed.jsonl` (checked by a test); style
  evaluation prompts are a separate list from the oracle teacher's prompts.
- **Why:** (1) A cap that no stream can meet would fail every run for a reason that says nothing
  about the clusters. (2) `03` has no section for benchmark shape and the config is for the
  system under test. (3) Without it (priority 1.0) a 50-cycle stream gave 5 to 9 `jargon` clusters on every seed
  and 7 to 12 `multi`, below the guard's minimum on most seeds (with it: `jargon` 14 to 19,
  `multi` 10 to 18; first run: `multi` 3 to 6 clusters even at
  200 cycles, which was a bug in the question builder: it dropped a pair if its port was ever
  updated, instead of from the update on). (4) A request that quoted the fact would plant the
  value in the ledger just after it was meant to be gone.
- **Evidence:** `tests/unit/test_synthetic_user.py` (50 tests; I-P11-1, 2, 6, 8, plus truth-table,
  gold-answer, forget-request, tool, style and oracle-row checks); 15 deliberate breakages each
  fail a named test (one first survived because the mutation did not change the cluster, and one
  needed a planted collision test). Cluster counts at 50 cycles, seeds 0 to 2: `jargon` 14 to 19,
  `multi` 10 to 18 (seed 2 at 10, the minimum), `style` 10, `fact` 28 to 41; at 200 cycles every
  category except `style` and `tool` has at least 26 clusters and a largest share of at most 8.7%.
- **Status:** decided.

### Fourth review: items not adopted as defaults
- **Per-module rank and a shared gate per attention layer (3C).** The premise is overstated: a
  rank-8 update of `q_proj` reaches every query head, and the 32B model has 40 heads, not 64.
  The question is still fair, so both variants are E-MLP arms.
- **Probe-aware pruning (runtime 2B).** Prune already runs the retention test on the slab's own
  probes before it commits (`P07` §3).
- **A fused custom kernel (runtime 1B).** Last in the list of remedies in `P04` §11, after
  measurement.
